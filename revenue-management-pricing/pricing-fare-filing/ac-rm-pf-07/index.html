Fare Rule and Penalty Structure Design

Fare rules and penalty structures, including change and cancellation fees, are designed to balance revenue protection against customer flexibility expectations and competitive positioning.

Air Canada contextA penalty structure only has commercial value if it is applied consistently everywhere a change or cancellation can be processed; an inconsistency between the contact centre and self-service becomes a customer complaint about fairness.
Trigger
A fare family redesign is undertaken, or a competitive or regulatory pressure requires the penalty structure to be reassessed.
Outcome
A defined penalty structure by fare family, filed and consistently applied across booking and servicing touchpoints.

Key performance indicators

  • Filed within the required lead time at 100 percent
  • Consistent application verified across all channels at 100 percent
  • Checked against CTA and destination-market rules at 100 percent
  • Audited quarterly for 100 percent of fare families

Risks and control points

  • Self-service and contact centre systems applying the same filed penalty rule differently
  • A single global penalty structure not fitting every market's regulatory constraints
  • An application inconsistency reaching the customer as an apparent fairness failure
  • Penalty rule logic being complex enough that filing errors are hard to catch before they apply
