Digital Content Publication with English and French Parity

Web and app content, including fare rules, policy pages and campaign content, is published and maintained with full English and French parity as required under the Official Languages Act.

Air Canada contextBilingual parity is a legal obligation on every customer-facing content flow, not a translation nicety, and content published in one language before the other is a compliance gap even if brief.
Trigger
A content update is required for a policy change, campaign launch or scheduled review.
Outcome
Published content live in both official languages simultaneously, accurate and consistent with the underlying policy or system state.

Key performance indicators

  • Both languages published within the same publication event at 100 percent
  • Validated for 100 percent of policy-related content before publication
  • Verified within 24 hours of publication at 100 percent
  • Zero confirmed parity gaps open for more than 24 hours

Risks and control points

  • Source content structurally authored in one language first, creating a risk of a parity gap
  • A publishing tool limitation causing one language to go live before the other despite the intent
  • A content error caught in one language not automatically being caught in the other
  • Manual parity verification not scaling well to a large and continuous content volume
