Mobility Aid Handling and Tracking

A customer's mobility aid, including wheelchairs and other assistive devices, is accepted, tracked and handled through the journey, from booking through arrival, under ATPDR obligations.

Air Canada contextIn-app mobility aid tracking launched in December 2023 specifically because this handling had been a significant source of customer harm and complaint; the ATPDR obligations here are not discretionary service quality, they are regulatory requirements.
Trigger
A customer with a mobility aid requirement books a flight or checks in for travel.
Outcome
The mobility aid correctly handled and tracked through every leg of the journey, delivered to the customer intact and on time at arrival.

Key performance indicators

  • Accepted and tagged within the standard check-in window at 100 percent
  • Delivered within 20 minutes of aircraft arrival at 95 percent
  • Status updated within 10 minutes of each handling milestone at 90 percent
  • Zero mobility aid damage incidents unreported beyond 1 hour

Risks and control points

  • A customer without their mobility aid at arrival facing immediate and serious personal impact
  • Battery-powered device handling documentation being incomplete under dangerous goods rules
  • Device damage being a higher-severity incident than baggage damage but not always handled with matching urgency
  • Device dimensions and battery details not being fully captured at the original booking
