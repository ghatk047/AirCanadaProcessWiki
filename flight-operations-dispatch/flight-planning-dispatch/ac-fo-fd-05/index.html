Payload and Weight Balance Load Planning

Final passenger, cargo and fuel weight is confirmed and the load plan calculated, feeding the loadsheet that governs the aircraft's centre of gravity for departure.

Air Canada contextLoad planning sits at the convergence of several independently changing inputs, passenger count, cargo weight and fuel load, arriving from different systems on their own timelines right up to the close of loading.
Trigger
Boarding and cargo loading approach completion ahead of a flight's scheduled departure.
Outcome
A calculated, validated load plan within centre of gravity limits, ready for loadsheet issuance.

Key performance indicators

  • Calculated within centre of gravity limits at 100 percent
  • Confirmed before final load calculation at 100 percent
  • Finalised before loadsheet issuance at 100 percent
  • Late load-change recalculation completed within the departure window at target rate

Risks and control points

  • Late passenger or cargo changes right up to door close affecting a calculation already in progress
  • Actual loaded weight differing from the planned weight communicated earlier
  • A fast recalculation under time pressure introducing an error close to departure
  • A finalised plan not matching actual loading, requiring correction before the aircraft can depart
