Express Operator Capacity Purchase Allocation

Express operator capacity purchased from Jazz Aviation and PAL Airlines is allocated across regional routes within the contracted capacity purchase agreement limits.

Air Canada contextExpress capacity is contractually fixed, not commercially flexible: it cannot absorb overflow from a mainline decision made elsewhere in the schedule, which makes allocation a hard constraint rather than an optimisation input.
Trigger
Seasonal schedule build reaches Express allocation, or a change in regional demand requires reallocation within the contract.
Outcome
Regional route capacity allocated to Express operators within contracted limits, feeding correctly into the mainline connecting banks.

Key performance indicators

  • Allocation within contracted capacity purchase agreement limits at 100 percent
  • Express feed aligned to mainline bank windows on 85 percent of connections
  • Type assignment matched to route requirements at 100 percent
  • Limits confirmed before allocation begins at 100 percent

Risks and control points

  • Regional demand exceeding contracted capacity with no immediate remedy available
  • Operator scheduling constraints conflicting with the mainline connecting bank window
  • Contract limits and actual operator fleet availability diverging without notice
  • Late regional demand shifts unable to be absorbed once allocation is committed
